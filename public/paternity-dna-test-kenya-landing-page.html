How a DNA Paternity Test Works

A DNA paternity test works by comparing specific genetic markers between a child and the alleged father. Every child inherits half of their DNA from their biological mother and half from their biological father. By analysing these genetic markers, the test can determine whether the man tested is the biological father.

  3. Sample collection — DNA samples are collected using a buccal (cheek) swab. This is a soft swab gently rubbed on the inside of the cheek. The process is painless, non-invasive, and takes only a few moments per person.
  4. Laboratory analysis — Samples are processed using established DNA analysis methods to compare the genetic profiles of the child and alleged father.

Who Needs to Participate in a Paternity DNA Test?

A standard paternity DNA test requires samples from the child and the alleged father. The mother's participation is optional but can strengthen the conclusiveness of the results.

Participant Required? Notes
Child Yes A sample is required from the child. Testing can be done for children of any age, including newborns.
Alleged Father Yes A sample is required from the man whose paternity is being tested. He must consent to the test.
Mother Optional (recommended) Including the mother's sample can strengthen the results, but the test can be conducted without her participation.

What if the alleged father is unavailable?

If the alleged father is unavailable, unwilling, or deceased, alternative DNA relationship tests may be able to help establish the biological connection. These include:

  • Grandparent DNA test — if the father's parents are available
  • Siblingship DNA test — if the father has other known children
  • Avuncular DNA test — if the father's brother or sister is available
  • Y-DNA paternal lineage test — for male-line testing where appropriate

Sample Requirements for a Paternity DNA Test

DNA samples for paternity testing are collected using a buccal (cheek) swab. This is the standard and most widely used method for DNA testing in Kenya.

How buccal swab collection works

  • A soft, cotton-tipped swab is gently rubbed on the inside of the cheek for a few seconds.
  • The process is completely painless and non-invasive — no needles or blood are involved.
  • Collection takes only a few moments per person.
  • The method is suitable for participants of all ages, including newborn babies.

Preparing for sample collection

  • Avoid eating, drinking (except water), chewing gum, or smoking for at least 30 minutes before sample collection.
  • Ensure hands are clean before handling any collection materials.
  • For legal tests, all participants must bring valid government-issued photo identification.

Sample collection options

  • At our facility — Sample collection at Biotel DNA in Nairobi.
  • Home DNA testing — Our trained sample collectors can come to your preferred location in Nairobi.
  • Other locations — We coordinate testing services in other parts of Kenya including Mombasa and Kisumu. Contact us to discuss availability in your area.
Important for legal tests: If you need your paternity test results for court, immigration, or any official purpose, you must arrange a legal DNA test before samples are collected. Legal tests require formal identification, authorised sample collection, and chain-of-custody procedures.

Private vs Legal Paternity DNA Testing in Kenya

When arranging a paternity DNA test in Kenya, it is important to choose the right type of test for your needs:

Private Paternity Test (Peace of Mind)

  • For personal knowledge only
  • No formal identification required
  • Simple sample collection process
  • Results are not admissible in court or for official purposes
  • Generally more affordable

Legal Paternity Test

  • Results can be used in court and for official purposes
  • Formal identification of all participants required
  • Chain-of-custody procedures followed throughout
  • Sample collection by authorised personnel
  • Full documentation provided
